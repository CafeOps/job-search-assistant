# Persona 3. Positioning

Goal: one page, `<data>/PERSONA.md`, that says who this person is for the jobs they want. The resume headline
and summary, the first paragraph of a letter, the LinkedIn headline and About, and the answer to "tell me
about yourself" are all cut from this page. Write it once, in the user's words, and reuse it.

Needs: TARGETS.md, STORIES.md, SKILLS.md, NUMBERS.md, and 5 to 10 real postings for the jobs they want (the
user picks them, or take them from the daily digest and PIPELINE.md).

## Steps

1. **Read the market.** From the postings, list the duties and skills that repeat, with how many postings
   ask for each. Mark each one: has it, with proof (name the story), has it but thin, or doesn't have it.
   Record this as the "Market map". The third group is never claimed. It goes to the gaps list.
2. **Pick the lane.** Ask first: "Of the work you've proven you can do, what do you want more of, and what
   do you never want to do again?" Then pick the one kind of job where their proof is strongest and where
   they want to go. Never build a lane on work they hate. A second lane only if the user needs it, and
   then it gets its own resume variant (`../03-resume-rules.md`).
3. **Three themes.** What they are known for. Each theme is a capability, two stories that show it, and a
   number or a scope. Use the contrast question from the interview: what do they do that others with the
   title don't?
4. **The level.** The honest one, from years in the lane and the HR-record titles in PROFILE.md. No seniority the
   years don't back.
5. **The one-liner.** "<Role or lane> who <does what> for <whom or where>, <one proof point>." Write three
   versions from their own phrases. They pick one or fix one.
6. **Resume headline.** "<Title> | <two or three strengths, in the postings' words>".
7. **Summary.** Two lines. Years, lane and setting, then what they build or run and the kind of result.
   Nothing a reader can't check.
8. **The spoken pitch.** 30 to 45 seconds, written for the ear: what I do now and at what scale, how I got
   here with one proof point, what I want next and why. The user says it aloud. Fix every place they stumble.
9. **Bios**, only if they speak, publish or freelance: one line (120 characters), short (50 words), long
   (120 words). Otherwise skip.
10. **What they are not.** Titles, skills and claims to stay away from, so later drafts don't drift.
11. **The proof map.** Each claim in the one-liner and themes, with the story and number behind it.
12. **Confirm.** The user approves the page. Add a line to `<data>/DECISIONS.md`.

## Tests

- **Swap.** Could the line describe anyone with the same title? Then it is too general. Add the specific.
- **Stranger.** Does someone outside their company and field follow it?
- **Two minutes.** Can they talk for two minutes on each theme, and name the proof?
- **Manager.** Would their manager nod at every line?
- **Banned list.** VOICE.md, every time.

## Done when

PERSONA.md has the market map, lane, themes, one-liner, headline, summary, pitch, bios (if any), the "not" list and
the proof map, and the user confirmed it. PROGRESS.md says `persona: positioning confirmed YYYY-MM-DD`.
Next: `4-resume.md`. The letter, profile and interview guides come after it.
