# Persona 4. The resume, from the fact base

This guide decides what goes on the page. `../03-resume-rules.md` owns the rules, the build, the quality gate
and the visual check. Run its resume pass when you finish here.

## Steps

1. **The frame.** Settle with the user: page count, sections and their order (experience first for most.
   Education first for a student or new graduate. Projects high for a career changer), bullets per job
   (Rule 2), which jobs get space (the last ten years. Older ones get a line each), and the title layers
   (Rule 3). Promotions at one employer sit under one employer heading, each title with its own dates, so
   the growth shows.
2. **The slate.** For each job, list every usable story as a candidate bullet with its lane, how many target
   postings ask for it (the market map in PERSONA.md), its strength (a measured result or a passed
   audit or inspection, then an estimate, then scope) and how recent it is. Pick the top ones so that no
   two cover the same lane. The first bullet of the current job is the strongest match to the lane. Show the user the picks and the bench in ONE message.
   They swap what they like.
3. **Write the bullets** from the story's own words. Shapes are in `../04-story-bank.md`. One proof point
   each. A number keeps its status word ("estimated"). Fit the width band. No internal names.
4. **One duties bullet** for the current job: a scope line that says what they run day to day, so the reader
   sees the job and not only the projects.
5. **Skills** come from SKILLS.md only. "Daily at work" and "project at work" go in. "Study or lab" goes in
   only if labelled and the user wants it. "Exposure" stays off. Three or four labelled lines, ordered by
   what the target postings ask for most, spelled as the postings spell them.
6. **Headline and summary** from PERSONA.md.
7. **Education, certifications, projects.** Exact names. An unfinished degree uses the wording in SCREEN.md.
   Held certifications only (Rule 7).
8. **Keywords.** Check the draft against the market map. Each repeated requirement the user truly has
   appears once in the posting's words, in a bullet or in Skills. One they lack is never added.
9. **Three reads.**
   - Ten seconds: does the top third say who they are, at what level, in which lane?
   - The hiring manager: for each bullet, what did they do, how big was it, what changed?
   - The interviewer: point at any line. Can the user talk about it for two minutes and name the proof? If
     not, cut it or reword it.
10. **Hand over** to the resume pass in `../03-resume-rules.md`: confirm the facts in one message, draft
    once, build, look at the page, one critic, the user, record.

## One resume, not one per posting

Fitting a resume to one posting means reordering bullets inside a job and swapping in a bench bullet. Never
a new fact. The cover letter does the rest. A separate variant file is only for a clearly different lane.

## What makes a resume weak

A list of duties with no result. A number with no unit or status. The team's work claimed as their own. A
tool list with no use behind it. Adjectives. Internal project names. A summary anyone could have written.
